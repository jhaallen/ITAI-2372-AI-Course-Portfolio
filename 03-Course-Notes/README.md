# Course Notes

This section summarizes several key concepts covered throughout ITAI 2372 and reflects how my understanding of artificial intelligence developed during the course.

## Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

Artificial intelligence refers to systems designed to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, such as recognizing patterns, making predictions, understanding language, and supporting decisions.

Machine learning is a branch of AI that allows systems to improve by analyzing data and identifying patterns rather than relying only on fixed instructions. The quality of the data, the model, and the human oversight all affect the quality of the results.

## Common AI Applications

AI is currently used across many industries to:

- Analyze large amounts of data
- Predict future outcomes
- Automate repetitive tasks
- Detect unusual patterns or risks
- Personalize customer and user experiences
- Improve operational decision-making
- Support communication and content creation

The value of AI depends on whether it solves a real problem and fits into the organization’s existing processes.

## Responsible and Ethical AI

AI systems can create risks when they are developed or used without proper oversight. Major concerns include:

- Bias and unfair outcomes
- Privacy and inappropriate data use
- Lack of transparency
- Inaccurate or misleading results
- Job displacement and workforce disruption
- Overreliance on automated decisions

Responsible AI requires human review, clear policies, appropriate data practices, transparency, and accountability.

## AI and the Workforce

AI is more likely to change many jobs than eliminate every job completely. Employees may spend less time on repetitive or administrative work and more time on judgment, communication, creativity, and decision-making.

Organizations must support employees through training, clear expectations, and opportunities to build new skills. Successful AI adoption depends on people understanding how to use the technology effectively and responsibly.

## AI Implementation

Implementing AI involves more than purchasing a tool. Organizations must first define the problem, identify the necessary data, evaluate risks, test the system, and decide where human review is required.

A practical implementation process includes:

1. Identifying a meaningful problem
2. Selecting an appropriate AI solution
3. Testing the solution in a controlled environment
4. Measuring accuracy and usefulness
5. Reviewing ethical and operational risks
6. Improving the system before expanding its use

## Key Takeaway

The course strengthened my understanding that AI should not be adopted simply because it is new or popular. It should be used intentionally to solve real problems, support people, and improve decisions while maintaining human accountability.
